### [14]

J. Slonaker, D. C. Motley, Q. Zhang, S. Townsend, C. Senatore, K. Iagnemma, and K. Kamrin, “General scaling relations for locomotion in granular media,” Physical Review E, vol. 95, no. 5, Art. no. 052901, 2017, doi: 10.1103/PhysRevE.95.052901.

**Access:** Publisher full text retrieved; abstract and Sections II–IV inspected. Publisher correction also inspected.

**Supported use:** RFT-based wheel dimensional model and conditional dynamic similarity; controlled physical wheel tests in one sand bed, with changing-gravity similarity assessed by DEM.

**Limit:** Does not supply universal cross-mechanism CoT, prescribed inchworm resistance/traction fractions, or physical experiments at Mars gravity. xi is a force-per-volume coefficient depending on grains, intruder roughness and gravity, not an arbitrary dimensionless fitted constant.

**Primary/institutional evidence:**

- [APS article](https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevE.95.052901): Author order, title, volume/article, publication May 10, 2017.
- [APS full text](https://harvest.aps.org/v2/journals/articles/10.1103/PhysRevE.95.052901/fulltext): Sections II–III, p. 052901-2: local RFT stress, grain-structure coefficient xi, product xi*D, dimensionless wheel power and velocity.
- [APS Publisher’s Note](https://harvest.aps.org/v2/journals/articles/10.1103/PhysRevE.95.069902/fulltext): Acknowledgment omission only; corrected June 1, 2017. No technical formula correction.

### [15]

C. Li, T. Zhang, and D. I. Goldman, “A terradynamics of legged locomotion on granular media,” Science, vol. 339, no. 6126, pp. 1408–1412, 2013, doi: 10.1126/science.1229163.

**Access:** Author-hosted article full text available and relevant RFT methods inspected; supplemental material not independently audited.

**Supported use:** Granular RFT background: depth-dependent local stresses and approximate additive integration for the studied leg geometries and intrusion conditions.

**Limit:** Does not independently validate helical three-dimensional stress superposition, a universal xi proportional to g in cohesive soils, or numerical inchworm factors chi=1/2 and lambda=4/3.

**Primary/institutional evidence:**

- [Author institutional publication record](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/a-terradynamics-of-legged-locomotion-on-granular-media): Title, author list, volume, issue, pages and DOI.
- [Author-hosted article and supplement](https://li.me.jhu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2013-Science.pdf): Main article pp. 1408–1409 describes direction/orientation/depth-dependent granular stresses and integrating surface-element forces.

### [16]

### [20]

A. Thoesen, T. McBryan, D. Mick, M. Green, J. Martia, and H. Marvi, “Granular scaling laws for helically driven dynamics,” Physical Review E, vol. 102, no. 3, Art. no. 032902, 2020, doi: 10.1103/PhysRevE.102.032902.

**Access:** Author-hosted full article read for model, geometry and measured/predicted Figure 4 data; plotted points reconstructed separately. No raw-author-trial files obtained.

**Supported use:** Helical within-family scaling and the public source of the reconstructed BP-1 comparison used in the source data, methods and Figure 7.

**Limit:** CoT invariance follows conditionally from the scaling transformation; this is not universal cross-design invariance. Geometrically similar wording is too strong because length scales as b/a^2 while radius/pitch scale as a. The same-study target prototypes are not independent external experiments. No Mars physical tests or raw trials were recovered.

**Primary/institutional evidence:**

- [APS publication record](https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevE.102.032902): Author list, title, journal, article and September 15, 2020 publication date.
- [ASU author-hosted article](https://labs.engineering.asu.edu/birth/wp-content/uploads/sites/190/2021/02/PhysRevE.102.032902.pdf): Eqs. (4)–(8) and Table I: helical scaling assumptions and prototype geometry. Figure 4: Earth BP-1 measured and predicted power/velocity. Reduced-gravity section uses DEM.

### [21]

Q. Zhang, S. Townsend, and K. Kamrin, “Expanded scaling relations for locomotion in sloped or cohesive granular beds,” Physical Review Fluids, vol. 5, no. 11, Art. no. 114301, 2020, doi: 10.1103/PhysRevFluids.5.114301.

**Access:** Publisher full text retrieved; abstract and Sections I–III/Eqs. (1)–(3) inspected.

**Supported use:** Limits and extensions of the original cohesionless scaling model. Cohesive or sloped beds require the relevant additional dimensionless conditions to be matched.

**Limit:** Does not mean every cohesive granular scaling is impossible. At fixed material, size and cohesion, changing g changes rho0*g*L/sigma_c and invalidates simple similarity unless compensated. Validation here is DEM, not new physical rover tests.

**Primary/institutional evidence:**

- [APS publication record](https://journals.aps.org/prfluids/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevFluids.5.114301): Three authors, Physical Review Fluids 5, 114301, November 3, 2020.
- [APS full text](https://harvest.aps.org/v2/journals/articles/10.1103/PhysRevFluids.5.114301/fulltext): pp. 114301-2–3: cohesive stress sigma_c, added group rho0*g*L/sigma_c and bed slope theta; verification via DEM using shaped wheels.
